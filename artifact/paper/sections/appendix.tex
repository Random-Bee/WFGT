\section{Drawing Affine Orders}
\label{sec:app-affine}

An affine order of $k \geq 2$ items (Section~\ref{sec:impl}) needs a stride $b$ coprime to $k$. Our implementation obtains a candidate stride in $1, \ldots, k-1$ from a random number by a remainder, which is close to uniform but slightly biased, and if it is not coprime to $k$, tries the next one up, until it finds one.

\begin{lemma}[Affine orders]
\label{lem:affine}
For $k \geq 2$, the search for a stride coprime to $k$ stops after at most $2\sqrt{k}$ tries, of $O(\log k)$ steps each, so it takes $O(\sqrt{k}\log k) \subseteq O(k)$ steps.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Euclid's algorithm tests a stride in $O(\log k)$ steps. The search never passes $k-1$, which is coprime to $k$, so it stops within $j(k)$ tries, where $j(k)$, Jacobsthal's function, is the least number such that any $j(k)$ consecutive integers include one coprime to $k$. Kanold showed that $j(k) \leq 2^{\omega(k)}$, where $\omega(k)$ is the number of distinct prime factors of $k$~\cite{kanold_jacobsthal}, and $2^{\omega(k)}$ is at most the number of divisors of $k$, which is at most $2\sqrt{k}$.
\end{proof}
